I. Executive Summary: Core Operating Metrics and Three Core Conclusions

1-1 Three Core Conclusions

1.Export value growth (+24.5% YTD) and maritime container throughput growth (+9.0% YTD) present a 2.7x structural scissors gap; avoid blindly chasing rates or locking capacity:
Vietnam’s national exports reached a record monthly high of USD 59.48 billion in September 2026 (MoM +8.5%, YoY +39.1%), with a highly concentrated export mix driven by high-value, FDI-oriented electronics, partly diverted to airfreight—“Computers, electronic products and components” alone generated USD 22.560 billion (YoY +112.7%), accounting for 37.93% of September’s total exports and 71.5% of the nationwide increase in export value. Including “Machinery and equipment” (9M: USD 55.013 billion, YoY +27.9%) and “Phones and parts” (9M: USD 51.768 billion, YoY +18.8%), the top three categories represented 53.06% of total national exports and contributed 77.7% of the cumulative increase in exports for the first nine months. By contrast, the recovery in traditional labor-intensive “textiles and garments” (9M: USD 30.323 billion, YoY +1.9%) and “footwear” (9M: USD 18.044 billion, YoY +1.4%) remained modest. Domestic enterprises’ exports declined seasonally by 9.1% MoM in September due to the seasonal transition of Central Highlands durian, although they still grew by +9.68% YoY. In addition, S&P Global’s Vietnam Manufacturing PMI edged down to 51.9 in September, with the “new export orders” subindex slowing for a second consecutive month. Physical container/FCL volumes have not doubled in line with trade values; procurement teams should avoid using headline export-value growth as the sole basis for aggressive freight-rate bidding across all trades.
2.Wan Hai’s September revenue reached a 50-month high and surpassed Yang Ming, while its global schedule reliability fell to 23.2%, creating Q4 delivery-delay risk:
Wan Hai (2615.TW) reported September revenue of NT$21.775 billion (MoM +13.41% / +NT$2.575 billion, YoY +97.63%), exceeding Yang Ming (NT$21.699 billion) for the first time. Structurally, intra-Asia routes accounted for 55% of total loaded volume but only 37% of total revenue. Earnings upside is primarily driven by the Middle East/India-Pakistan trade, which contributes 27% of revenue, and the Americas long-haul trade, which contributes 36% (Wan Hai’s Q2 Gross Margin reached 30.37%, the highest among the three major Taiwanese carriers). However, according to Sea-Intelligence’s global liner schedule-reliability report for August, published on September 29, 2026, Wan Hai’s global schedule reliability declined from 35.6% in June and 29.8% in July to 23.2% in August, ranking last among the world’s 13 largest carriers and trailing the global average of 49.9% by 26.7 percentage points. The primary cause is congestion across Wan Hai’s regional fleet, particularly in East China—where port congestion is more severe given Shanghai’s 12.2% schedule reliability and Ningbo’s 16.9%—and at Singapore and Malaysia transshipment hubs. Upstream congestion can trigger cascading schedule slippage at Ho Chi Minh City’s Cat Lai terminal. For urgent, high-value shipments or orders subject to service-level agreement (SLA) penalties, priority deployment should be given to the Gemini Cooperation, an operating alliance comprising European carriers Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd, which achieved a combined schedule reliability of 77.8% in July–August, with Maersk at 67.3% and Hapag-Lloyd at 61.5%; MSC (70.1%); Ocean Alliance, comprising Evergreen and other partners (48.3%); or point-to-point services offered by TS Lines (TSL) / IAL. For bookings on Wan Hai vessels, allow a 3–5-day delivery buffer in the vessel schedule and request a delay-related rate concession during negotiations.
3.Procurement strategy recommendations for Q4 2026 through early 2027: “Lock in long-term rates on the India-Pakistan trade during the off-season, pass through surcharges in a tiered manner on Middle East routes and secure capacity ahead of the overlapping peak-season holidays, remain selective on intra-Asia routes despite elevated rates, and pursue coordinated, two-way heavy-container Matchback negotiations across the Group’s import and export flows”:
•India-Pakistan trade: Indian Diwali-related ocean freight stocking demand concluded in mid-October, and India’s west coast ports (Nhava Sheva / Mundra), located outside the Strait of Hormuz conflict zone, have absorbed overflow mother-vessel capacity seeking to avoid Middle East risks, including new capacity from the 1,200-meter berth at Cai Mep’s TCTT-CMIT facility, which commenced commercial operations on 10/09. The spot rate from Ho Chi Minh City to India’s west coast is USD 600–850 per 20'DC (all-in: approximately USD 875–1,205). The India-Pakistan trade enters its post-holiday off-season from mid-November. Negotiations to lock in Q4 2026–Q1 2027 contract rates should be completed before the end of October, with carriers’ requests for November General Rate Increases (GRI) surcharges declined.
•Middle East Route: Strait of Hormuz risk remains elevated (100 security incidents cumulatively reported as of 10/09, with reports of three more container vessels attacked in mid-October); on 10/16, the SCFI Persian Gulf route reached USD 6,474/TEU (peak of USD 6,586 on 9/24), creating an 8x price spread versus the India-Pakistan route. For CIF quotations to Middle East destinations (including transshipment at the outer Gulf of Oman ports of Khor Fakkan and Sohar), Maersk's Tier 3 Emergency Conflict Surcharge (USD 1,800 for 20'DC dry containers; USD 3,000 for 40'DC/HC dry containers; and USD 3,800 for 20'/40' Reefer / DG / OOG containers), CMA CGM's standard surcharge (USD 2,000 for 20' / USD 3,000 for 40'), and the Strait transit fee (USD 1,000) should be itemized separately and passed through to buyers on an actual-cost basis. In addition, as the 2027 Lunar New Year (2027/02/06) and the Islamic holy month of Ramadan (with the fast expected to begin on 2027/02/08) fall only two days apart, creating a "dual-holiday overlap effect", container allocation should be secured before 2026/11/20 for the mid-December to late-January peak season.
•Intra-Asia Routes and Bidirectional Heavy-Container Linkage: on 10/16, the SCFI Korea route had retreated 8.06% from its 9/18 high of USD 397 to USD 365/TEU; the Southeast Asia route edged down to USD 1,156/TEU; and Drewry's IACI was revised down 3.3% to USD 1,453/FEU. As intra-Asia freight rates appear to have peaked and are losing momentum, weekly spot negotiations are recommended for Q4 rather than locking in rates at peak levels. Given that Vietnam imported USD 187.34 billion from China, USD 69.30 billion from South Korea, and USD 49.90 billion from ASEAN during the first nine months, and that container devanning in southern Vietnam has generated substantial volumes of empty containers on return legs, the group should consider combining its "Southern China / Taiwan / Japan and South Korea → Ho Chi Minh City" inbound raw-material heavy-container volumes with its "Ho Chi Minh City → Asia / India-Pakistan" outbound finished-goods heavy-container volumes in a joint tender. This would facilitate negotiation of a bidirectional heavy-container linkage contract (Round-trip Matchback Contract), with the objective of securing a Container Imbalance Charge (CIC) waiver and a 10%–15% discount on return-leg freight rates.

1.20'DC (20-foot standard dry container, internal capacity 33.2 CBM, road weight limit 20.0–25.0 metric tons):

Conversely, 4,250–8,500 TEU feeder or mother vessels serving India, Pakistan and the Middle East berthing at Cai Mep Deepwater Port (14.0–16.5 meters of water depth) require high-density placement of laden 20'DC containers in the lower holds as ballast cargo, keeping the base ocean freight rate for 20'DC shipments from Ho Chi Minh City to India's west coast within USD 600–850.
2.40'HC (40-foot high-cube container, internal capacity 76.3 CBM, providing 13% more capacity than a standard 40'DC):
•Core Cargo Sources: computers and electronics (USD 123.659 billion), machinery and equipment (USD 55.013 billion), mobile phone components (USD 51.768 billion), textiles and garments (USD 30.323 billion), footwear (USD 18.044 billion), automobile and motorcycle parts (USD 15.082 billion), wooden furniture (USD 13.300 billion), tires and plastic products.
•Two-Way Heavy-Container Closure and Free Time Negotiations: Given Vietnam's DVA of 27% for electronics, 36% for textiles and 34% for footwear, more than USD 280 billion of electronic components, fabrics and machinery imported from China, South Korea, Taiwan and Japan during the first nine months are predominantly delivered in laden 40'HC containers. After devanning, the empty containers remain in Binh Duong's Song Thuan ICD, Dong Nai's Long Binh ICD and the Ho Chi Minh City High-Tech Zone, providing Vietnamese exporters with ample 40'HC empty-container availability. In addition to seeking exemption from container imbalance charges (CIC) during contract negotiations, the procurement function should apply to Wan Hai, TS Lines and Yang Ming for laden 40'HC shipments to India, where customs clearance commonly takes 7–14 days, and the Middle East, where onward inland transit is time-consuming, for a combined 14–21-day free detention and demurrage period at destination (Combined Free Detention & Demurrage) (standard: 5–7 days), reducing exposure to detention charges of USD 80–150 per container per day.
3.40'RH (40-foot refrigerated high-cube container, 67.5 CBM internal capacity) and DG hazardous-cargo containers:
•Core Cargo Sources and Structural Deficit: Seafood (USD 9.100 Billion ($B)) and tropical fruits and vegetables (USD 7.200 Billion ($B)) primarily rely on 40'RH equipment. Because Vietnam imports relatively little refrigerated cargo, Ho Chi Minh 40'RH containers have maintained a long-term equipment deficit, with outflows consistently exceeding inflows. Carriers must reposition empty reefer containers from Hong Kong and Singapore to Cat Lai, while feeder vessels have limited reefer plug capacity. During the peak season, shippers are advised to reserve empty containers and plug quotas 10–14 days in advance.
•New hazardous-cargo container (DG) developments: New Saigon Port (SNP) updated its hazardous-cargo container acceptance policies for Cat Lai and Hiep Phuoc Port on September 11, 2026, including limits on on-port dwell days for specified IMO classifications and mandatory Direct Loading at the vessel. 【Why does the Ho Chi Minh–India rate stand at only USD 600–850, while the Persian Gulf rate reaches USD 6,474–6,586—an eightfold difference? —— Three underlying structural drivers】

1.Geographic and theater-of-conflict segmentation: The Strait of Hormuz controls the sole maritime gateway to the Persian Gulf (affecting Jebel Ali, Dammam, Hamad, and Shuwaikh). According to International Maritime Organization (IMO) data, from the escalation of the Middle East conflict in late February 2026 through October 9, 100 confirmed vessel safety incidents have occurred across the Middle East and the Strait of Hormuz, resulting in 24 crew fatalities. Three container vessels were reportedly attacked in mid-October, requiring vessels entering the Strait to bear an additional war-risk insurance premium of 0.5%–1.0% of hull value and ECS of USD 1,800–3,800. By contrast, India’s west coast (Nhava Sheva and Mundra) and Pakistan (Karachi) are located on the open Arabian Sea, requiring no transit through either the Strait of Hormuz or the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, and therefore fall within zero-war-risk-surcharge trading lanes.
2.Excess Capacity Spillover Suppresses India–Pakistan Freight Rates (Capacity Spillover Effect): As global carriers reduce direct calls to the Persian Gulf due to regional risk, 4,000–8,500 TEU mother ships previously serving the Far East–Middle East Persian Gulf trade are using India’s west coast (Mundra / Nhava Sheva) and the Gulf of Oman’s outer ports (Sohar / Khor Fakkan) as turnaround destinations. Although the Indian subcontinent has replenishment demand during the September–October Diwali peak season, it is also absorbing risk-driven excess capacity, creating an 8x rate differential between high Middle East rates and lower India–Pakistan rates.
3.Backhaul Ballast Pricing for Heavy Containers: Ho Chi Minh City and Cai Mep offer high-density 20'DC cargoes, including coffee, rice, plastic pellets, and metals. To fill ballast slots in the lower tiers, mother ships accept India–Pakistan 20'DC heavy containers in the Southern Vietnam market at marginal rates.
